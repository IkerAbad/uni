Speed and Performance

  • HDD: It is the slowest of the three options. Since it relies on mechanical components (rotating platters and a read/write head that searches for data), it has higher latency and limited transfer speeds, typically between 80 and 160 MB/s.
  • SSD: It offers the highest performance. Since it operates through electrical impulses without moving parts, data access is almost instantaneous, exceeding 500 MB/s on SATA models and reaching over 3,000 MB/s on NVMe PCIe drives.
  • Flash Drive: It falls between HDDs and SSDs. Although it uses the same flash memory technology as SSDs, its simpler controllers and the limitations of USB connectors or card readers result in more moderate speeds.

Capacity and Price

  • HDD: It is the most economical option per gigabyte and allows the largest storage capacities (from 1 TB to over 20 TB) while keeping costs low.
  • SSD: It has the highest cost per gigabyte. Although models with several terabytes are available, matching the capacity of a mechanical hard drive usually requires a significantly larger investment.
  • Flash Drive: It maintains a relatively affordable price for everyday use but is generally aimed at lower capacities (typically between 32 GB and 512 GB, reaching up to 1 TB in high-end models).

Durability and Portability

  • HDD: It is the most delicate and bulky option. A shock or strong vibration while the platters are spinning can damage the read/write head and cause data loss. In addition, its size (2.5" or 3.5") and weight make transportation less convenient.
  • SSD: Since it has no moving parts, it is highly resistant to shocks and vibrations. It is lightweight and compact, although an external SSD still requires an enclosure and connection cable.
  • Flash Drive: It is the clear winner in portability. It withstands impacts with ease, and its tiny size allows a USB drive or SD card to be carried in a pocket without additional cables.
