What Is External Storage?

External storage refers to a group of devices that store information outside the computer and can be connected or disconnected whenever needed. They are not part of the computer's internal memory; instead, they are added to expand storage space or to move data from one place to another.

They are usually connected through a port, most commonly USB, and the operating system recognizes them as another storage drive. They can hold anything from a school project to a complete disk backup.

Why Storing Information Matters

Everything we do on a computer generates data, and that data can be lost. An internal drive may fail, malicious software may delete files, or a physical impact may damage the computer. Keeping a copy on a separate storage device is the simplest way to avoid losing everything.

They are also useful for carrying information with you and opening it on another computer without requiring an internet connection.

The Three Types We Will Study

  • Magnetic hard drives (HDD): store data on rotating platters and offer large storage capacity at a low cost.
  • Flash memory: USB flash drives and SD cards, compact and durable devices designed for carrying files.
  • Solid-state drives (SSD): contain no moving parts, making them much faster.

No single technology is the best in every situation. The right choice depends on how much data needs to be stored, how quickly it must be transferred, and the available budget. In the comparison section, you can see them side by side.
